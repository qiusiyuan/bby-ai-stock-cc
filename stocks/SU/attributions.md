### 2026-08-04 · -1.92% day · ▼ moderate
**Tags:** `geopolitical_war`, `macro_oil`, `commodity_move`, `earnings_pre_print`, `policy_us`
**Confidence:** high

**Primary cause.** US and Qatar signalled progress on a draft US-Iran agreement, reviving hopes that Strait of Hormuz risk recedes. WTI fell -5.63% to $75.82 and Brent back to about $80, unwinding nearly all of July's war premium (crude 30d now only +1.2% versus a +25.9% peak on 7/23). SU fell only -1.92% — far less than crude — because Q2 earnings were due after the close tonight and those numbers reflect the high-oil-price quarter, so traders held into the print.

**Sources.**
- Yahoo: [Oil Extends Losses After US, Qatar Signal Progress on Iran Draft Deal](https://finance.yahoo.com/)
- Investor's Business Daily/Yahoo: [Stock Market Today: Dow Pops 1,000 Points On Hormuz Talks](https://finance.yahoo.com/)
- Euronews/Yahoo: [BP profit more than doubles as Middle East conflict boosts oil prices](https://finance.yahoo.com/)
- _Corroboration:_ Whole energy complex declined but far less than crude: SU -1.92%, CVX -1.09%, CNQ -0.10%, HXE.TO -2.59%. HXE.TO was the ONLY energy name with elevated volume (volr 1.39x), suggesting allocator-level selling versus trader-level holding in single names.

**Cross-assets.** SPY +2.02% · VIX 16.34 · TEN YEAR 4.627 · WTI 75.82

**Agent read.** The critical read today is that energy's small decline is LAG, not RESILIENCE, and the distinction is measurable. Crude has given back 96% of its July war premium (30d from +25.9% peak to +1.2%) while SU has given back under 15% of its own (peak +17.5% to +15.0%). If crude stabilizes near $75, fair value for the oil-and-gas group is roughly 30d +3-6% reflecting genuine earnings improvement rather than geopolitical premium, which implies about 8-12% of catch-down risk in SU, 6-9% in CNQ, and 3-6% in CVX. Three reasons today's decline was muted, two of them temporary: earnings-window protection (SU tonight, CNQ 8/6, and the sector print pattern is already confirmed by BP profit doubling and Exxon posting its best quarter in four years), the deal being unsigned so the market still prices a collapse probability, and genuinely improved cash flow (CVX Q2 revenue +56% YoY) which is the one durable factor. The single most informative detail is that HXE.TO, the Canadian energy ETF, was the only energy name trading on elevated volume (1.39x) while single names were quiet (SU 0.97x, CVX 0.73x, CNQ 0.60x) — ETFs typically reflect allocator flows and single names reflect trader flows, so allocators appear to be reducing energy while traders wait for earnings. Allocators are usually earlier. Scenario split over 2-4 weeks: deal signed 45% (SU $57-60), stalemate 40% (SU $62-67), talks collapse 15% (SU $70-75). Framing for the user: this is not a sell recommendation, it is the factual observation that the SOURCE of energy's 30-day excess return is disappearing — good earnings tonight would be a rear-view mirror confirmation of a price environment that no longer exists.
